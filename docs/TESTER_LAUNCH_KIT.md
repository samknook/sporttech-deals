Date: 4 November 2026

## Purpose
Test whether real first-time and developing triathletes can benefit from:
- 70.3 Readiness Check
- 70.3 Race Pack preview
- Blueprint free chapters

**Important:** This is not a paid pre-order, a coaching service, or a newsletter signup. No user feedback or purchase intent has been observed yet.

## Suggested pilot structure
- Ten opt-in testers: 4 first-timers, 3 Sprint/Olympic finishers who plan a 70.3, 3 experienced 70.3 athletes.
- Ask for at least six mobile users; include Android and iPhone, and a couple of desktop testers.
- Do not collect or publish health information, exact location or race performance details.
- Observe whether each tester (1) can enter `530` as a target, (2) understand the three pace scenarios, (3) can find a checklist, (4) can generate an export, and (5) knows what a future paid product would add.
- Let them use the six-question scorecard. If they want to send it, they use the email draft deliberately.
- Record problems and requests anonymously, without collecting email addresses in an analytics sheet. Email addresses naturally sent through voluntary feedback are only for correspondence and should be handled with an appropriate retention and privacy policy before systematic processing.

## Evidence-based launch gates
- Confirm live GitHub Pages and successful QA on iPhone, then run test panel.
- At least 8/10 users can complete the main race-planning flow unassisted.
- A majority can clearly explain the **additional** paid value rather than confusing it with the free prototype.
- Gather specific evidence of willingness to buy, without presenting hypothetical positive answers as actual pre-orders or revenue.
- Prioritize fixes supported by tester problems before new functionality.
- Before any paid launch: complete review of training/nutrition claims, digital delivery, consumer rights/privacy and legal business details, and verify payment process.

## Commercial strategy
An email from a tester is not a sale or a lead subscription. Do not silently add them to a marketing list. Avoid unsolicited mass messages, fake testimonials or urgency. The aim is to learn which user outcome matters most, then deliver it.
